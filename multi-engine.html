Why the Baron

The twin operators actually fly.

Most multi add-ons happen in a Duchess or a Seminole — good trainers you'll never fly again. A Baron is the airplane charter, corporate and owner-flown operators actually run. Time in it reads differently in a logbook, on a resume, and to an insurer.

A real conventional twin

Two 260-hp Continental IO-470s, a critical engine, and single-engine performance a 180-hp trainer can't show you. You learn engine-out flying the way it actually works — not the softened version a counter-rotating Duchess teaches.

An IFR panel you'll see again

Garmin GTN 750 navigator with a classic six-pack, so you fly real approaches on one engine. An engine monitoring system and new fuel sending units are being added.

Based at Monroe (D73)

No Class B traffic, a 5,000-ft runway, and self-serve fuel well under PDK prices. More of your hour goes to flying.

Checkrides without the wait

Adler works with examiners who have openings, so a finished syllabus doesn't sit for weeks waiting on a ride.

The aircraft

A 1963 Beech 95-B55 with two fuel-injected Continental IO-470-L engines. Props left, throttles center, mixtures right — the layout you'll be briefed on before your first flight, because it's where Barons catch pilots coming from other twins.

Common questions

Before you add the multi.

Is there a written test?
No. A multi-engine add-on has no knowledge test — just training and a checkride with an oral.
How many hours will it take?
The add-on is 6.5 hours of training flights plus the checkride, flown at a pace that fits your schedule — most pilots finish in a few weeks of regular lessons. Pilots coming from singles often need one to two extra hours in a Baron; extra time is billed at the same hourly rates.
What do I need before day one?
A private or commercial certificate and a current medical. You'll get the N145X study guide when you enroll; before the first ground lesson you'll memorize its speed card, the engine-failure flow and the takeoff brief — arrive without them and the lesson is rescheduled.
Do I need high-performance and complex endorsements?
The Baron requires both, and both are included in the add-on course. If you already hold them, great — nothing changes.
Can I rent the Baron solo afterward?
That depends on insurance requirements for time in type. Ask — you'll get a straight answer on exactly what it takes.
Where do we fly from?
Monroe–Walton County Airport (D73) in Monroe, Georgia, about 45 minutes east of Atlanta. Single-engine training runs out of PDK and LZU.
What about weather?
Airwork needs ceilings around 4,500 ft or better at Monroe. Weather scrubs are never charged — the lesson just moves.
